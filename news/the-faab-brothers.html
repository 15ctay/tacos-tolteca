The FAAB Brothers: Two Empty Wallets, $200 Spent, and a Backup Ravens QB Who Never Had a Chance

The FAAB Brothers

Two managers. Two empty wallets. $200 of FAB, gone. One of them is in last place. The other just took over the power rankings. They are not the same kind of broke.

Editor's note: ctay, who runs this site, asked us to write this. The desk stays neutral on his games. The desk is not staying neutral on his budget. Every figure below comes from Yahoo: standings and FAB remaining as of Tuesday, Nov 10, the current transaction log (which begins Sep 30), and the commish's September bids from the same Yahoo log we used in the Oct 10 FAB roast. One early ctay claim is missing from that window; we flag it instead of guessing.

Welcome to Tacos Tolteca, a league with a $100 FAB budget, 12 teams, and now four managers who have spent every last dollar. Two of those broke teams are sitting in 10th and 4th and are not the subject of this column. The other two are Ima Burrow Into Polly (ABMCommissioner, 6-3, No. 1 in the power rankings) and Coker!? I barely know her (ctay, 2-7, 12th place).

They have the same remaining balance. They do not have the same story.

How the Money Actually Left

The Commish: broke by Week 3, then invented a new economy

$77 on Marcus Mariota. In Week 3. In a 2-QB league, spending on a quarterback is not automatically a crime. Spending seventy-seven dollars, then cutting him 13 days later, is not a bid. It is a cover charge.

From that morning on, the commish has been shopping with the lights off. Every later claim in the log is a $0 waiver or a free agent. He has 36 transactions from Sep 10 through Nov 6, including the Metcalf trade. Sixteen different quarterbacks have passed through this roster: the two who never appear as adds and are still starting for him (Joe Burrow, Brock Purdy), the two he opened the waiver season by cutting (J.J. McCarthy, Mac Jones), and then Jameis Winston, Cooper Rush, Carson Wentz, Joe Flacco, Marcus Mariota, Easton Stick, Cade Klubnik, Joe Fagnano, Kyle Allen, Tanner McKee, Andy Dalton and Joe Milton. Mac Jones even came back, like a boomerang that did not learn.

And yet. He is 6-3. He has the best two-week form in the league (166.69). He posted the top score in Week 4 (174.54), Week 8 (171.36) and Week 9 (162.02). He is No. 1 in the power rankings. Woody Marks, whom EQ cut on Nov 1, is in his Week 10 flex. DK Metcalf, acquired for a $0 quarterback the commish had already cut once, is in his starting lineup. The $100 is gone, and none of the four players he paid for are still on the team. The roster that is winning games was assembled, in large part, after the money ran out.

This is not fiscal responsibility. This is a man who set his wallet on fire in September and has been cooking on the open flame ever since.

The Fagnano Problem

Joe Fagnano is the connective tissue of this league's bad decisions, and both brothers have held him.

  • Oct 11, 4:50pm: The commish adds Fagnano off free agency.
  • Oct 12, 5:26am: The commish drops Fagnano for Kyle Allen. Time of possession: 12 hours and 36 minutes. Cost: $0.
  • Nov 4, 1:33am: ctay claims Fagnano for $66, his entire remaining budget, and cuts J.K. Dobbins to do it.
  • Nov 4, 1:20pm: ctay trades Fagnano, plus the $16 Jonah Coleman, to GettinGoff in Dan for Stevenson and McConkey. Time of possession: 11 hours and 47 minutes. Cost: $66.

The commish owned Joe Fagnano for free for slightly longer than ctay owned him for sixty-six dollars. EQ now has Fagnano in a QB slot for Week 10, which means the only manager who might actually start this player is the one who did not spend a dollar to get him.

That same night, 27 minutes after the $66 claim, ctay added Carson Beck off free agency. By sunrise, last place had two new backup quarterbacks. By lunch, he had traded one of them. Beck is still on the bench. The backup's backup is the one who stayed.

Also at 1:35am that morning: Bears D in, Steelers D out, which did fix the Week 9 Steelers bye the preview had already flagged. The Bears are on bye in Week 10, and they are still in the starting lineup. ctay patched a hole by moving it one week to the right. The wallet is empty, the defense is off, and waiver claims now cost money he does not have.

Closing Arguments

The FAAB Brothers are a single statistical fact — two managers, $0 remaining — wrapped around two completely different crimes.

The commish committed his in public, early, and all at once. He put 77% of a season on Marcus Mariota, went broke before some teams had used the waiver wire as anything but furniture, and then spent the next seven weeks turning $0 claims and other people's leftovers into a 6-3 roster, a three-game win streak, and the No. 1 power ranking. It should not have worked. It is working. We published a whole column about this in October, and he responded by keeping it up.

ctay committed his in private, late, and in a 12-hour window we will be mentioning until the trade deadline. He sat on a lead in the "still has money" standings into November, then spent his last $66 on a quarterback the commish had already sampled for free, traded him before dinner, and is now 2-7, out of FAB, and heading into Week 10 with a defense on bye and a flex running back on a plantaris. The five-game losing streak is over, which is the official good news. The official bad news is that the next waiver claim is going to have to be free, and this league has seen what "free" looks like when last place is the one shopping.

One brother went broke and built a team anyway. The other went broke and built a trade that arrived pre-injured. Same bank balance. Very different credit scores.

The waiver wire resets every Wednesday. The budget does not. See you at the $0 bin, boys. It's going to be crowded, and two of you already live there.
